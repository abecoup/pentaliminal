PWhat can you use?

Four products for using AI without giving up your judgment.

Each one answers a question people now face with AI. They share one memory underneath, so what you learn in one carries into the others.

None of these is available yet. The screens below show what each product is being designed to do. The people, companies and figures in them are made up.

  1. T Trust Can we prove what our AI did? For companies running AI agents on customer or financial records. Being designedNow
  2. N Notebook Can I learn and use what I know? For students, researchers and writers: anyone who works with ideas. Under constructionNext
  3. R Rhetor Can I say it well? For anyone who has to speak well: students, teachers, professionals. PlanningLater
  4. F Forum Where do we reason through hard questions together? For academics, teachers, students and curious listeners. PlanningLater

Rhetor helps you say it well. Forum is where it gets said. Notebook keeps what you learn. Trust checks the AI work that comes out of all of it.

TPentaliminal Trust

Being designedNow

When someone asks what your AI did, you’ll have the answer.

Trust keeps one record of every AI action in your company, whether a person typed the prompt or an agent acted on its own. Each step links back to the request that started it. Outside AI is kept out, and your own agents stay inside the limits you set.

Proof, provenance and protection

  • Every prompt and agent step, in one record

    Who asked, which tool or model answered, what came back, what changed, and who approved it.

    Proof

  • Trace any change back to its cause

    Agents call tools, read files and hand work to other agents. Each step links to the person and request that started it.

    Provenance

  • Keep out AI you didn’t invite

    Unwanted crawlers and agents are stopped at the door, with a plain report of what tried to get in.

    Protection

  • Your agents ask before risky steps

    Each agent gets an identity, permissions and stop-and-ask rules, like “ask before changing totals.”

    Protection

  • Hand an auditor the whole story

    Audit packets with inputs, outputs and sign-offs, plus usage by team, person and agent.

    Reports

  • Fair to the people being recorded

    People can see their own records. Reports are team-level by default, and opening one person’s detail needs a stated reason, which is logged too.

    Privacy

What you get
A full answer to “what did our AI do, and who asked it to?” for any day, any agent and any person.
How you can check it
Every step links to its cause. Any record exports as an audit packet with inputs, outputs and sign-offs.

NPentaliminal Notebook

Under constructionNext

A notebook that helps you remember what you read, and use it.

Bring in your notes, papers, chats and talks. Notebook links them as you write, brings old ideas back when they matter, and checks that you actually know what you saved.

Five jobs, in any order

  • Bring everything in

    Notes from other apps, papers, chats and recorded talks. Read and listen right where they land.

    Gather

  • Search by what you remember

    Try “that long argument in August that never got settled.” Move through ideas by how they connect, not by folder.

    Find

  • Links that suggest themselves

    AI proposes links and tags as you write, and you accept or ignore them. Links you never use fade.

    Connect

  • Know it, not just save it

    Guess before you see the answer, recall it again on a schedule, and say how sure you are, so you can see where you’re fooling yourself.

    Learn

  • Old ideas, back when you need them

    Notes resurface beside whatever you’re writing. Gaps between ideas show up as open questions.

    Apply

  • Honest about what you know

    Claims carry a status, and you’re warned when a new one contradicts something you wrote before.

MHow do they fit together?

Everything you bring in becomes one shared memory.

Notes, agent runs, talks and debates are kept the same way: as four kinds of record that every product reads and writes. A debate’s claims, a student’s notes on them and a reviewer’s sign-off stay linked, not scattered across three apps.

Shared memory

  • Question

    What someone is trying to find out or decide, sharpened over time.

    “Should we adopt this tool?” becomes “Will it cut review time without hurting accuracy?”

  • Claim

    A statement with its source and a status.

    KnownInferredAssumedDisputedUnknown

  • Trace

    The path a piece of work took: what was asked, what was tried, where it turned. A person’s thinking or an agent’s actions.

  • Review

    Who checked what, against which question, with what verdict, and how far the reviewers agreed.

Later, we plan to open this memory as a standard other AI systems can build on.

01Why wisdom?

Intelligence is not the same as wisdom.

Artificial intelligence is expanding what can be done. But capability alone does not tell us what is worth doing. A system can solve a problem efficiently without asking whether it is the right problem.

Capability

answers what can be done.

Wisdom

helps determine what ought to be done, and when, why, and with what degree of confidence.

What we mean by wise intelligence

Intelligence directed by sound judgment: informed by context, honest about uncertainty, attentive to multiple perspectives, conscious of consequences, and aimed at ends that hold up under thoughtful scrutiny.

This is a working definition and a research ambition. We don’t claim wisdom can be reduced to a score or fully captured in software.

The most important question is not how intelligent we can make our machines. It is how wisely we can put intelligence to use. From the founding manifesto

04How do we look at a problem?

Five lenses. A threshold between worlds.

“Penta” is the five intellectual traditions behind our work. “Liminal” is the threshold: between the familiar and the emerging, between human and machine intelligence, between knowledge and judgment.

No single lens sees everything. Choose one to see the ground it covers, and what it leaves for the others.

  • 01

    How does intelligence work?

    Perception, attention, memory, learning, reasoning, metacognition, consciousness, and decision-making.

  • 02

    What moves human beings?

    Motivation, emotion, development, behavior, social cognition, relationships, and well-being.

  • 03

    How do humans live together?

    Culture, institutions, social structures, power, ritual, collective behavior, and diverse ways of life.

  • 04

    What is true, valuable, and worth doing?

    Epistemology, practical wisdom, virtue, ethics, meaning, and competing accounts of the good.

  • 05

    How do societies and systems evolve?

    Historical patterns, complexity, feedback, political economy, technological change, and long-term consequences.

Engineering is the translational discipline. The five lenses inform the work; engineering turns it into architectures, software, protocols, evaluations, and products.

05What does wisdom take?

What wisdom asks of a system.

A working framework that runs from knowing to acting. It is a synthesis to be tested and refined, not a settled taxonomy of wisdom.

  1. 01

    Knowing

    Epistemic humility

    Can the system tell evidence from assumption, communicate uncertainty, and revise its conclusions when warranted?

  2. 02

    Seeing

    Perspective-taking

    Can it consider different stakeholders, interpretations, and cultures, including perspectives that are easy to overlook?

  3. 03

    Situating

    Contextual understanding

    Can it account for history, relationships, institutions, incentives, and the wider environment a decision will operate in?

  4. 04

    Valuing

    Value discernment

    Can it identify what is at stake, surface competing values, and make ethical tensions and trade-offs explicit?

  5. 05

    Acting

    Practical judgment

    Can it choose proportionate actions, weigh consequences, and recognize when to defer or seek human input?

06Which problems are worth solving?

From solutionism to problem wisdom.

Problem selection is itself an act of judgment. What we measure, whose experience we include, and what we count as success shape an intervention as much as the technology does.

Conventional approach

Pentaliminal approach

What is worth building, and why?

How we weigh a problem

  • Importance

    How deeply does the problem affect human lives, institutions, or living systems?

  • Scale and distribution

    Who is affected, how widely, and who bears the greatest burden?

  • Tractability

    What interventions are plausible, and what evidence supports them?

  • Neglectedness

    Where is attention or capacity notably missing?

  • Leverage

    Could progress here unlock progress across other domains?

  • Risks and externalities

    What could an intervention worsen, displace, or make harder to reverse?

  • Time horizon

    What are the near-term effects, and what might the long-term consequences be?

07What are we building?

Infrastructure in three parts.

Connected, not a rigid stack. Model-agnostic and composable, built to work across models, agents, applications, and organizations. The systems underneath will change. The need for context, judgment, trust, and responsible action will remain.

  • 01

    Orientation

    Helps systems understand what they are trying to accomplish, and whether that goal is well formed: problem formulation, priorities, values, stakeholders, and assumptions.

  • 02

    Cognitive

    Helps systems understand the world and regulate their own reasoning: contextual memory, provenance, world models, uncertainty, and noticing what is missing.

  • 03

    Agency

    Helps systems turn judgment into responsible action: identity, delegation, permissions, verification, human oversight, escalation, and accountability.

The ambition is not to put one “wise AI” in charge. It is to make wiser behavior more achievable across many intelligent systems.

08How do we build it?

Principles for building.

Start with the ends

Technology is a means. Begin with the human need, then ask whether technology is the right intervention.

Treat wisdom as a practice

A system is not wise because it sounds confident. Define observable behaviors, test them, study failures.

Make uncertainty visible

Separate what is known, inferred, assumed, disputed, and unknown. Confidence should be earned.

Respect pluralism

Human values differ and sometimes conflict. Surface the trade-offs instead of hiding them behind false neutrality.

Preserve human agency

Help people understand, choose, create, and act. Don’t quietly replace their judgment.

Design for consequence

Consider who is affected, what happens downstream, what can be reversed, and what could go wrong.

Make trust operational

Permissions, provenance, verification, and human control are design requirements, not afterthoughts.

Learn from the world

Judge systems by real outcomes and lived experience, not internal benchmarks or polished demos.

Think past the near term

Consider future generations, institutions, ecosystems, and what happens when intelligence is concentrated or shared.

09Where does the work happen?

Pentaliminal Labs

Labs is where we study cognition and wisdom, test design hypotheses, develop evaluation methods, and prototype infrastructure.

We publish research and essays, build experimental systems, and contribute open-source work where it helps. Research informs what we build, and building shows us what we still don’t understand.

The future of intelligence is not about machines that do more. It is about systems that help us understand what is worth doing.

Pentaliminal exists to work at that threshold, with scientific curiosity, philosophical seriousness, and engineering discipline.

You’ll find more questions than answers here. That’s on purpose: the questions are where the work starts, and we’d rather work on them with you.
