PWhat can you use?

Four products for using AI without giving up your judgment.

Each one answers a question people now face with AI. They share one memory underneath, so what you learn in one carries into the others.

None of these is available yet. The screens below show what each product is being designed to do. The people, companies and figures in them are made up.

  1. T Trust Can we prove what our AI did? For companies running AI agents on customer or financial records. {{CHIP:trust}}{{HZ:trust}}
  2. N Notebook Can I learn and use what I know? For students, researchers and writers: anyone who works with ideas. {{CHIP:notebook}}{{HZ:notebook}}
  3. R Rhetor Can I say it well? For anyone who has to speak well: students, teachers, professionals. {{CHIP:rhetor}}{{HZ:rhetor}}
  4. F Forum Where do we reason through hard questions together? For academics, teachers, students and curious listeners. {{CHIP:forum}}{{HZ:forum}}

Rhetor helps you say it well. Forum is where it gets said. Notebook keeps what you learn. Trust checks the AI work that comes out of all of it.

TPentaliminal Trust

{{CHIP:trust}}{{HZ:trust}}

When someone asks what your AI did, you’ll have the answer.

Trust keeps one record of every AI action in your company, whether a person typed the prompt or an agent acted on its own. Each step links back to the request that started it. Outside AI is kept out, and your own agents stay inside the limits you set.

Proof, provenance and protection

  • Every prompt and agent step, in one record

    Who asked, which tool or model answered, what came back, what changed, and who approved it.

    Proof

  • Trace any change back to its cause

    Agents call tools, read files and hand work to other agents. Each step links to the person and request that started it.

    Provenance

  • Keep out AI you didn’t invite

    Unwanted crawlers and agents are stopped at the door, with a plain report of what tried to get in.

    Protection

  • Your agents ask before risky steps

    Each agent gets an identity, permissions and stop-and-ask rules, like “ask before changing totals.”

    Protection

  • Hand an auditor the whole story

    Audit packets with inputs, outputs and sign-offs, plus usage by team, person and agent.

    Reports

  • Fair to the people being recorded

    People can see their own records. Reports are team-level by default, and opening one person’s detail needs a stated reason, which is logged too.

    Privacy

What you get
A full answer to “what did our AI do, and who asked it to?” for any day, any agent and any person.
How you can check it
Every step links to its cause. Any record exports as an audit packet with inputs, outputs and sign-offs.
What it asks of you
Connecting the agents and AI tools you already use. Nothing records screens or keystrokes.
How we’ll know it works
Design partners pay for it. The first version is the agent ledger and one audit report.

NPentaliminal Notebook

{{CHIP:notebook}}{{HZ:notebook}}

A notebook that helps you remember what you read, and use it.

Bring in your notes, papers, chats and talks. Notebook links them as you write, brings old ideas back when they matter, and checks that you actually know what you saved.

Five jobs, in any order

  • Bring everything in

    Notes from other apps, papers, chats and recorded talks. Read and listen right where they land.

    Gather

  • Search by what you remember

    Try “that long argument in August that never got settled.” Move through ideas by how they connect, not by folder.

    Find

  • Links that suggest themselves

    AI proposes links and tags as you write, and you accept or ignore them. Links you never use fade.

    Connect

  • Know it, not just save it

    Guess before you see the answer, recall it again on a schedule, and say how sure you are, so you can see where you’re fooling yourself.

    Learn

  • Old ideas, back when you need them

    Notes resurface beside whatever you’re writing. Gaps between ideas show up as open questions.

    Apply

  • Honest about what you know

    Claims carry a status, and you’re warned when a new one contradicts something you wrote before.

What you get
What you save sticks, and shows up when you’re working on something that needs it.

Recall checks show what you know versus what you only saved. Every claim keeps its source.

People come back daily and remember more. The first version: import, search by memory, resurfacing while you write, and the learn mode.

RPentaliminal Rhetor

{{CHIP:rhetor}}{{HZ:rhetor}}

Practice speaking the way musicians practice.

Pick a speaker you admire. Rhetor breaks down how they do it, has you try a passage side by side with your camera, and loops the hardest ten seconds until it sticks.

Study, imitate, compare, drill

  • 01

    See how a great speaker does it

    A blueprint of their words, sentence shapes, moves, structure and delivery.

    Study

  • 02

    Try it side by side

    Your camera next to the model, one passage at a time.

    Imitate

  • 03

    Feedback on one skill at a time

    Compared with the model on rhythm and delivery, and on whether your message landed. Not a dozen numbers at once.

    Compare

  • 04

    Loop the hard part

    Repeat the hardest ten seconds, the way a musician works on a tricky bar.

    Drill

  • Persuasion that rests on evidence

    Rhetor teaches the moves that help a true point land, and names the tricks that work without evidence.

    Holds a line

What you get
Talks, lessons and pitches that land, in a voice that’s still yours.
How you can check it
Each recording is compared with the model, and with your own recordings from a month ago.
What it asks of you
A camera, and short sessions on one skill at a time.
How we’ll know it works
People’s recordings measurably improve over a month. The first version: a small library of annotated speeches, side-by-side practice, one-skill sessions and passage loops.

FPentaliminal Forum

{{CHIP:forum}}{{HZ:forum}}

Hear the best arguments on a hard question, live or later.

Debates, talks and podcasts among teachers, researchers and students. Every session is kept with a transcript and chapters, and tied to the question it argues, so you can follow a disagreement across many conversations.

Live, kept, mapped, followed

  • Debates, talks and rooms

    Live debates, chat rooms around a question, and streamed talks from conferences and universities.

    Live

  • Nothing vanishes after the weekend

    Every session is recorded, transcribed, split into chapters and searchable.

    Kept

  • See exactly where people disagree

    Each conversation ties to a question. Its claims are mapped: positions, objections, and what changed by the end.

    Mapped

  • Follow a question, not a feed

    Track one question across every conversation about it. “Show me the strongest case on the other side” is a normal way to browse.

    Followed

  • Nothing goes public without everyone’s yes

    A recording is published only when everyone in it agrees.

    Consent first

What you get
The strongest case on each side of a hard question, without hunting through scattered podcasts and feeds.
How you can check it
Every mapped claim links to the moment it was said.
What it asks of you
A link. Listen live, or later with chapters and a transcript.
How we’ll know it works
Listeners come back for the depth. The first version: one field (AI and philosophy), a few hosts, one conference stream.

MHow do they fit together?

Everything you bring in becomes one shared memory.

Notes, agent runs, talks and debates are kept the same way: as four kinds of record that every product reads and writes. A debate’s claims, a student’s notes on them and a reviewer’s sign-off stay linked, not scattered across three apps.

Shared memory

  • Question

    What someone is trying to find out or decide, sharpened over time.

    “Should we adopt this tool?” becomes “Will it cut review time without hurting accuracy?”

  • Claim

    A statement with its source and a status.

    KnownInferredAssumedDisputedUnknown

  • Trace

    The path a piece of work took: what was asked, what was tried, where it turned. A person’s thinking or an agent’s actions.

  • Review

    Who checked what, against which question, with what verdict, and how far the reviewers agreed.

Later, we plan to open this memory as a standard other AI systems can build on.
